<h2 class="section-head">Tokenized Equities Face Their First Major Issuer Pushback</h2>
<p>The SEC's September innovation exemption created a pathway for regulated secondary trading of tokenized US equities through new Tokenized Securities Venues (TSVs). The framework allows these venues to operate permissioned onchain trading pools outside the traditional Regulation NMS system, subject to requirements including KYC checks, trading limits and market-wide halts. However, it also gives issuers the right to object when a third party seeks to tokenize their shares. That provision is now being tested: at least 16 companies, including Microsoft, Apple, Google, Goldman Sachs, Broadcom and Nasdaq, have objected to their shares being listed on OKX-ICE's proposed platform. A subsequent account put the number of objections at 56 by the end of the notification period.</p>
<p>The dispute highlights a fundamental divide in the tokenization market. Exchanges and platforms pursuing third-party tokenization argue that investors primarily want convenient access to the economic exposure of public equities. In contrast, issuer-sponsored platforms maintain that tokenized securities should preserve the full legal, economic and governance rights attached to the underlying shares. The SEC's framework also creates a hybrid model: public blockchain infrastructure, but permissioned access and regulatory oversight. The challenge will be whether compliant US venues can offer a sufficiently compelling experience compared with offshore alternatives, while demonstrating that tokenization delivers more than simply a new format for existing securities.</p>

<h2 class="section-head">ICE Brings Hyperliquid Closer to US Institutional Markets</h2>
<p>Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), the parent company of the New York Stock Exchange, has signed a definitive agreement with Trade[XYZ] to bring Hyperliquid-based perpetual futures to US customers, subject to regulatory approval. ICE will operate the regulated exchange and clearing infrastructure, while Trade[XYZ] will design and deploy the markets using Hyperliquid's HIP-3 technology. This permissioned extension of Hyperliquid allows market operators to restrict trading to approved participants through onchain allowlists. The planned venue is expected to offer perpetual contracts on equities, cryptocurrencies, indices, commodities and foreign exchange. ICE has also made a strategic investment in Trade[XYZ], with investor Stan Druckenmiller publicly confirming his participation.</p>
<p>The announcement is significant because it suggests that established financial institutions may increasingly use decentralized trading infrastructure rather than build competing systems from scratch. Hyperliquid provides the underlying technology, while regulated intermediaries take responsibility for market operations, customer access and clearing. The new US venue will operate separately from existing permissionless HIP-3 markets, meaning liquidity will initially be fragmented across different order books. Its launch also remains dependent on regulatory approval, and important questions remain around risk management and the relationship between ICE's clearinghouse and Hyperliquid's onchain liquidation engine. Nevertheless, following Payward's earlier commitment to HIP-3, ICE's involvement is a strong signal that institutional adoption of onchain derivatives is moving beyond experimentation.</p>

<h2 class="section-head">Record Crypto Exploits Expose the Weakest Links in Security Infrastructure</h2>
<p>Binance suffered the largest crypto theft on record, with attackers draining approximately $6.3 billion from its hot wallets across ten blockchains. The FBI attributed the attack to North Korea's TraderTraitor group, part of the Lazarus network. According to Binance's incident report, attackers compromised employee devices through malicious software disguised as coding tools and assignments, eventually gaining access to internal systems used to deploy software and authorize transactions. They then manipulated Binance's own signing infrastructure to execute withdrawals, bypassing normal approval controls. Stablecoin issuers and Arbitrum's Security Council intervened to freeze or recover approximately $2.85 billion, around 45% of the stolen funds. Binance has stated that it will cover the remaining losses using company funds.</p>
<p>The incident reinforces a broader pattern across the industry: attackers are increasingly targeting the systems and people surrounding private keys rather than breaking cryptography itself. In October, Ledger also investigated approximately $91 million in losses after a hardware implant was discovered in devices sold through an authorized Southeast Asian reseller. The implant captured users' recovery phrases during setup, leaving the device's Secure Element and genuine-device checks untouched. Meanwhile, the PHP2Shell vulnerabilities demonstrated how AI-assisted research can accelerate the discovery of critical software flaws, with attackers attempting exploitation at unprecedented speed. Taken together with earlier incidents affecting Coldcard, Liquid and Bitget, these events underline that security must extend across the entire supply chain, from software dependencies and internal access controls to hardware distribution and operational procedures.</p>

<h2 class="section-head">US Regulators Advance Crypto Rules Across Derivatives, Prediction Markets and Custody</h2>
<p>October saw the CFTC take a more active role in defining the regulatory framework for digital asset markets. The agency opened a rulemaking process on leveraged retail crypto trading, exploring a tailored registration category for crypto asset markets and clarifying when transactions involving leverage or margin fall under its jurisdiction. The proposal could bring custodial exchanges offering leveraged products closer to federal registration requirements, while distinguishing these from onchain transactions that result in actual delivery of assets. The CFTC also issued an interim rule clarifying the treatment of certain casino-style gambling products and proposed a framework for event contracts covering sports, politics, culture and weather. Separately, the agency approved US exchanges to offer perpetual futures contracts linked to individual stocks, adding another potential building block for onchain and traditional derivatives markets.</p>
<p>The SEC also advanced its work on digital asset custody through a 760-page proposal that would allow state-chartered trust companies to serve as qualified custodians for investment advisers and funds, subject to due diligence and control requirements. It would also permit advisers to self-custody certain assets where no eligible custodian is available, provided they meet strict safeguards. Together, these developments point towards a more defined regulatory environment, with agencies increasingly seeking to establish pathways for crypto products within existing financial market structures. For the industry, the direction is becoming clearer: regulated access and institutional participation are expanding, but the rules governing who operates markets and how assets are held will remain central to adoption.</p>
